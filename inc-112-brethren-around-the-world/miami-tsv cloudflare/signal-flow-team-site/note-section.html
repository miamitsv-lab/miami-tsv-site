Signal Flow: Improving Sound for Worship Services

A Practical Guide for TSV

TermMeaning / Note
Allowed after lockAccuracy fixes, readability fixes, missing assets, light timing improvements, and minor clarity improvements.
Animation production passThe active build stage where finalized scenes are animated, exported, reviewed, and handed off for editing.
Animation scene listA checklist of every animated scene, diagram, label, avatar movement, and transition needed.
Avatar movementSimple host/guest movement such as mouth movement, blinking, and small head nods.
CalloutsArrows, boxes, or short notes pointing to important signal flow details.
chibi animationA simplified character-based animation style used to explain the audio path, equipment interaction, and practical worship-service examples through consistent characters and scenes.
Device graphic assetsMicrophone, mixer, processor, speaker, WebEx/streaming, and supporting graphics used consistently in diagrams and chibi scenes.
Diagram labelsShort text used to identify microphones, mixers, cables, outputs, speakers, and WebEx feed.
Final animation scene listThe approved scene checklist. Use it as the build map so the team stays on schedule and avoids late creative changes.
First reviewable animation batchA small set of completed priority scenes exported with clear names and version numbers for technical and editing review before the full rough cut.
Lower thirdsOn-screen name and role graphics placed near the bottom of the frame. In the Signal Flow project, they identify the host, guest, voice talent, or technical speaker without interrupting the animation or diagram explanation.
Narration timing anchorThe completed voice-over is used to determine scene length, movement timing, captions, callouts, and transitions.
Pre-production lockAfter the lock, do not add a new format, new visual style, major script rewrite, or added scenes unless approved as necessary.
Rough-cut assemblyThe first complete beginning-to-end edit combining the title card, chibi scenes, diagrams, narration, captions, and closing card for review.
Run-of-showThe full order of the video from opening to closing.
Section-card templatesReusable transition graphics that introduce major parts of the video. For the Signal Flow project, they can separate topics such as audio source and microphone, mixer and processing, output and speakers, troubleshooting, and the closing summary.
Signal flow pathThe audio path from microphone/input through mixer/processing and out to speakers/WebEx/streaming.
Simple production ruleKeep diagrams readable and use simple movement. Clarity is more important than complex animation.
StoryboardA scene-by-scene guide showing visuals, graphics, avatar scenes, transitions, and timing.
Title cardThe opening graphic that displays the project title and establishes the approved TSV visual identity before the host-and-guest explanation and chibi animation begin.
Visual proof testA short test to confirm the diagram style, avatar movement, labels, and readability before full production.
Voice-over synchronizationMatching each visual, label, callout, and scene transition to the corresponding spoken narration.
Water-pipe analogyThe illustration should connect water source, valve/control, and sprinkler output to microphone/source, mixer/processors, and speaker/output. It must stay simple, readable, and technically accurate.
